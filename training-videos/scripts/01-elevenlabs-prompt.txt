[confident] Welcome to ScanMaster Training. In the next three minutes, you'll understand the entire field of ultrasonic testing — without touching the software yet.

This is video one. No prerequisites. Let's start.

Ultrasonic testing — U-T for short — is how engineers see inside solid metal.

Think of it like sonar for a submarine, but instead of the ocean, we're scanning a jet engine, a pipeline, or a steel plate.

Sound waves go in. Echoes come back. The echoes tell us if there's a crack, a void, or just clean material.

Every UT inspection has exactly four players. Let's meet them.

One: the pulser. A small electronic box that fires high-voltage pulses. In ScanMaster, this is the R-P-P-3.

Two: the transducer. The probe that converts those electric pulses into actual sound waves — and listens for the echoes coming back.

Three: the part under test. Could be a plate, a tube, a forging — anything you need to verify.

And four: the receiver and display — in ScanMaster, the U-P-R 100 board and the M-C-I-O software you're learning to use.

These four players, working together, give you the A-scan — the most important graph you'll ever read.

[confident] U-T matters because it's non-destructive.

The part stays whole. It goes back into service. And you've proven it's safe to fly, to pressurize, to carry load.

Every aerospace primary structure, every nuclear pressure vessel, every offshore weld — all checked this way.

Every inspection you'll ever run follows the same five steps. Memorize this list — it's the spine of every video that follows.

Step one: set up the timebase and the material. Tell the system what you're scanning and how deep to look.

Step two: configure the pulser and the receiver. Pick the right frequency, the right voltage, the right filter.

Step three: tune the gain — and if your part is thick, add T-C-G — Time Corrected Gain.

Step four: place gates over the signals that matter. Gates are how you decide what's a pass and what's a fail.

Step five: save the setup, run the scan, review the result.

One mistake beginners make: they jump straight to the gates.

But if your timebase is wrong, your material velocity is wrong, or your gain is wrong —

[serious] — then every gate you place is measuring nonsense. The order matters.

U-T is sonar for solids.

Four players: pulser, transducer, part, and receiver.

Five steps, always in order.
