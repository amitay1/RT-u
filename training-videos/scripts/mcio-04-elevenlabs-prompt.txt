[matter-of-fact] Five settings here. Get one wrong, your signal is garbage.

Let's tune them right.

Inspection mode. Receiver filter. Amplitude. Damping. Pulser filter. P-R-F. Pulse width. Sub-pulses. Charge time.

Eight settings. Two tabs.

Receiver tab first. Three inspection modes.

Pulse echo — P-E. A single transducer connected to the pulser.

Through transmission — T-T. Two transducers — one transmitter, one receiver.

Dual. A dual transducer connected to the pulser.

One technical note from the manual: Dual mode's timebase range is half that of T-T mode.

Receiver filter. Same tab.

The rule from the manual: pick the filter closest to the centre frequency of your receiving transducer.

Click the value, drag the knob — your choice. The selected filter turns from blue to red.

Now Pulser tab.

Amplitude. Scale of one to eight. One is minimum, eight is maximum.

The manual recommends amplitude of five or greater. Less instrument gain is then required for a particular sensitivity, reducing the signal-to-noise ratio.

Damping Resistance. Controls the pulser's capacity to limit noise. Also one to eight.

Click the value or drag the knob. Selected value turns blue to red.

Two simple rules, big impact on every signal you'll ever see.

The Pulser also has a filter. A low-pass pre-amp filter designed to improve near-surface resolution. Two values.

One — low pass under one point five megahertz.

Two — low pass under three megahertz. The manual notes this displays increased near-surface resolution ability.

P-R-F. Pulse Repetition Frequency. The pace of the pulses.

Current P-R-F appears in the field. Minimum and maximum are displayed at the lower edge.

Three ways to set it. Combo boxes, type a value, or drag the gray knob.

One important rule. Your value must stay between the displayed minimum and maximum.

If you have more than one pulser, check the Ext checkbox. An external device then synchronises the pulse timing.

Pulse width. The duration of the pulse, in nanoseconds.

The manual's recommendation: set pulse width to twenty-five per cent of the transducer's frequency.

Combo boxes, type, or knob.

[serious] A warning. Increasing pulse width demands more electrical energy and charge time. If exceeded, an error appears in the Messages area and the system auto-adjusts to the nearest safe value.

Sub-pulses. The R-P-P 3 pulser delivers up to four sub-pulses within one P-R-F cycle.

Use case from the manual: highly attenuative materials. Sub-pulses increase the forcing frequency of the transducer, delivering higher ultrasonic energy into the part.

Middle of the Pulser tab, under Pulses, pick from the dropdown.

Charge time. Two ways to set it — automatic or manual. The automatic option is recommended.

Automatic. Check the Auto checkbox at the bottom right of the Pulser tab. The system sets charge time to optimum based on amplitude, pulse width, and sub-pulses.

Manual. Clear the Auto checkbox. Combo boxes or type a value in microseconds.

Out of range? The value defaults to the last acceptable value. Re-enter.

One final note from the manual. If you ever see "P-R-F value out of power limit" in the Messages area —

— lower one or more of: sub-pulses, pulse width, or P-R-F itself.

Recap.

Three modes. Filter at probe centre frequency. Amplitude five or higher. Pulse width about twenty-five per cent of probe frequency. Sub-pulses for attenuative materials. Charge time on auto.
