[matter-of-fact] Your material isn't in the list. You need to add it.

Or your velocity is wrong. You need to fix it.

Both, here.

[confident] Welcome to MCI/O Training. You'll add a new material, edit an existing one, delete one, and calculate a velocity from a known sample thickness.

All from the Material dialog boxes inside the Timebase tab.

From the right side of the Timebase tab, under Material, click Edit.

The New Material dialog box appears.

Every material in the current database, with velocity columns in millimetres per microsecond and inches per microsecond.

Adding a material. Five steps.

One. Select the material that you want to be listed immediately before the new one, then click New.

Two. A blank row appears after the material you selected. In the Material column, enter the name of the new material.

Three. In the blank row, double-click a field under one of the velocity columns. A velocity field and combo buttons appear.

Four. Use the combo buttons or type a value, then press Enter. The other velocity column auto-calculates.

Five. From the bottom of the dialog, click Save.

Editing — two paths.

To change the name. Double-click the Material column field. A cursor appears. Type your changes.

To change the velocity. Double-click a velocity column field. The value highlights blue, combo buttons appear. Type or click the new value, press Enter. The other column auto-calculates.

Click Save to commit.

Deleting. From the right side of the Timebase tab, under Material, click Edit.

Select the material to delete. Click Delete. The row disappears.

Click Save. The material is gone from the database.

The real magic. Calculate a material's velocity from a known sample thickness.

From the right side of the Timebase tab, under Material, click Cal. The Material Velocity slash Thickness Calibration dialog opens.

At the top, select the Velocity radio button. The Velocity field at the bottom becomes disabled — the system will calculate it.

Set six fields.

Gate number — first and second gates used for the calculation.

Offset — only when using a single gate, leave zero for two-gate calibration.

Mult, short for Multiple — the number of multiple signals between the gate pair.

Average — the number of pulses to average. Standard calibration is one.

Material — pick from the database.

Thickness — the measured thickness of the sample.

Click Cal. The calculated material velocity appears in the Velocity field.

Click Save to write the new velocity back to the database.

The mirror operation. Calculate thickness from a known velocity.

Same dialog. Same six fields. But at the top, select the Thickness radio button. The Thickness field disables.

Click Cal. Thickness appears.

One technique to get the most accurate calibration.

When distinct multiple signals are available — for example, multiple backwall echoes — increase the range between them.

Calibrating between a first and third backwall produces a more accurate result than between a first and second.

Quick recap.

Add, edit, and delete materials from the Material dialog box.

Calibrate velocity or thickness with the Material Velocity slash Thickness Calibration dialog.

Widen your gate spread for better calibration resolution.
